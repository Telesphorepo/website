Make the repositories your team publishes to, add proxies for the public registries your builds use, and say who can download and who can publish. Each repository has an **Access** tab with a **Download** card and a **Publish** card, and that tab is where you give and take away access.

## Make a repository

<Place where="In Telesphorepo" />

<Steps>

1. Open **Settings** › **Repositories**, and click **New repository**.

2. Under **What to create**, choose **A repository**.

3. Type a **Name**, such as `releases`. Builds and clients use the name in the address of the repository.

4. Under **Format**, choose the format, such as **Maven / Gradle** or **npm**.

5. Under **Who can download**, choose **Private** or **Public**. A public repository lets anyone download without signing in, and publishing still needs access.

6. Click **Create repository**.

</Steps>

<Done>Done when the new repository opens on its **Use this repository** tab, which shows the setup for each client. [Repository addresses](/repository-addresses/) lists the address of every format.</Done>

### Make a Docker repository

Make it in the same way, and choose **Docker** as the format. It holds container images, Helm charts and other OCI files. Its name, such as `docker`, is the first part of every image name.

Docker needs the server at the root of a host, such as `https://repo.example.com`. For layers over 100 MB behind Cloudflare, see [Push large Docker images](/push-large-images/).

## Give people Download or Publish

The **Access** tab has two cards. **Download** lists who can read the repository, and **Publish** lists who can add packages to it. A person who can publish can also download. The person needs an account first, so make one on [People and sign-in](/people-and-sign-in/) if they have none.

### Let someone download

<Place where="In Telesphorepo, on the Access tab of the repository" />

<Steps>

1. Open **Settings** › **Repositories**, click the repository, then click its **Access** tab.

2. In the **Download** card, under **Who can download**, click **Add**. A dialog opens with **Available** on the left and **Giving access** on the right.

   If the card shows only a **Public** switch, the repository is public and anyone can download. Turn **Public** off first to choose people.

3. In **Available**, type the start of a name in the search field. Click the person to move them to **Giving access**. Repeat for each person.

4. Click the button at the bottom, which counts what you chose, such as **Add 2 items**.

</Steps>

<Done>Done when the person is a row under **Who can download**, marked **Person**.</Done>

### Let someone publish

<Place where="In Telesphorepo, on the Access tab of the repository" />

<Steps>

1. Open **Settings** › **Repositories**, click the repository, then click its **Access** tab.

2. In the **Publish** card, under **Who can publish**, click **Add**. A dialog opens with **Available** on the left and **Giving access** on the right.

3. In **Available**, type the start of a name in the search field. Click the person to move them to **Giving access**. Repeat for each person.

4. Check **Permissions for new items**, at the top of **Giving access**. It starts with **Download** and **Publish** ticked, and that is what a publisher needs. To give one person something else, click their card and change its boxes.

5. Click the button at the bottom, such as **Add 2 items**.

</Steps>

<Done>Done when the person is a row under **Who can publish**.</Done>

Send a new publisher [Install the CLI](/install-cli/), and a person who only downloads [Use packages in your builds](/use-packages/).

To change what a person can do on the repository, click their name in either card, change the boxes in the dialog, and click **Save permissions**. To remove access, click the **×** on their row and confirm. **Delete files**, **Edit settings**, **Manage access** and **Manage CI pipelines** are the other repository permissions, and they appear as boxes in the same dialog.

:::note
A group or a proxy takes no uploads, so its **Publish** card says that nobody can publish.
:::

## Add public packages

A **proxy** keeps a copy of each package that people download from a public registry. A **group** puts several repositories behind one address, so people get your repository and a proxy through one address.

<Place where="In Telesphorepo" />

<Steps>

1. Open **Settings** › **Repositories**, click **New repository**, and choose **A proxy of a remote repository**.

2. Type a **Name**, choose the format, and under **Remote** click a well-known remote, such as **Maven Central** or **npm registry**.

3. Click **Create proxy**.

4. Click **New repository** again, and choose **A group of repositories**.

5. Type a **Name**, choose the same format, and under **Members** tick your repository and the proxy. The group reads its members in the order of the numbers beside them, so tick your own repositories first.

6. Click **Create group**.

</Steps>

<Done>Done when the group opens on its **Overview** tab. Point your builds at the address of the group.</Done>

In an npm, Python, Cargo or NuGet group, the first member that has a package name owns that name, so a public package cannot take the place of your private one.

## Add a CI pipeline

A CI pipeline is a rule on the server that says which CI job may publish to which repositories, so the job stores no secret. A developer sends you the values, and [Publish from CI](/publish-from-ci/#ask-for-a-ci-pipeline) tells them which ones.

<Place where="In Telesphorepo" who="For someone who can manage CI pipelines" />

<Steps>

1. Open **Settings** › **CI pipelines**, and click **New CI pipeline**. A form opens in numbered parts.

2. Under **Where the job runs**, choose **GitHub Actions** or **Jenkins**.

3. In the next part, **Which runs can publish** for GitHub or **Which Jenkins jobs can publish** for Jenkins, fill in the form from the developer's message. Each line of the message starts with the name of its field, such as **GitHub repository** or **Jenkins address**.

   | Field | What goes in it |
   |---|---|
   | **GitHub repository** | The repository, such as `acme/app` |
   | **Workflow file** | The file in `.github/workflows` that publishes, such as `publish.yml`. Empty lets every workflow of the repository publish |
   | **Branches** | The branches that may publish, such as `main`. A `*` matches any text |
   | **Jenkins address** | The address that people open Jenkins at |
   | **Jobs** | Each full job name, folders included, such as `team/app` |

4. Under **Repositories it can publish to**, tick the repositories that the message lists.

5. Click **Create CI pipeline**.

</Steps>

<Done>Done when **Settings** › **CI pipelines** lists the new pipeline. The job can then publish to those repositories, and each repository shows it in its **Publish** card.</Done>

### Jenkins: a freestyle job or a Jenkinsfile

Both kinds of job use the same CI pipeline. Write each full job name with its folders under **Jobs**, such as `team/app`. A branch of a multibranch pipeline is its own job, such as `app/main`.

## Let a network download without signing in

A private repository can let downloads in from chosen networks with no sign-in, such as the build machines of your office or the runners of GitHub Actions. An address only grants downloads. Publishing always needs access.

<Place where="In Telesphorepo, on the Access tab of the repository" />

<Steps>

1. Open **Settings** › **Repositories**, click the repository, then click its **Access** tab.

2. In the **Download** card, under **Who can download**, click **Add**.

3. In **Available**, click a network list, such as **GitHub Actions**. Or click **Custom address**, then type an address or a range such as `203.0.113.0/24` in the card that appears under **Giving access**, with a label if you like. To allow the address you are using now, click **Add my address**.

4. Click the button at the bottom, such as **Add 1 item**.

</Steps>

<Done>Done when the network list or the address is a row under **Who can download**. Click **×** on the row to remove it.</Done>

:::note
Public and networks do not go together. Turning **Public** on asks first, then removes the networks.
:::

### Network lists

A network list is a named set of addresses that several repositories can use. Open **Settings** › **Networks** to see them.

- **Downloaded lists**: Cloudflare and GitHub Actions. The server refreshes them every day.
- **Your lists**: click **New network list**, and paste addresses in any layout.

A change to a list applies to every repository that uses it.

## Troubleshooting

<details>
<summary>A proxy cannot reach its remote</summary>

The server checks the remote address when you create the proxy. Check the address. To create the proxy anyway, click **Create anyway**.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Jenkins jobs cannot publish, and the server cannot reach Jenkins</summary>

Open the Jenkins address followed by `/oidc/jwks`. Paste the whole page into **Jenkins keys** on the CI pipeline, and do it again when Jenkins changes its keys.

</details>

<details>
<summary>A network that you added still has to sign in</summary>

The server sees the address of your web server in place of the address of each visitor, so no network matches. **Activity** shows the address that each request came from. Tell the server to [trust the proxy](/run-the-server/#trust-the-proxy), and every visitor gets their own address.

</details>
